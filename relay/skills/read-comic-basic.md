These are consecutive slices of a comic chapter (manga, manhwa or webtoon). An app will read the text aloud to the reader.

For each slice, list every piece of story text: speech bubbles, thought bubbles, narration boxes, captions and character name or stat boxes.
- Put them in natural reading order: top to bottom, and in the reading direction given below within the same row.
- Copy the words faithfully, but write them in normal sentence case instead of all caps. Join the lines of one bubble into one sentence and fix words hyphenated across line breaks.
- Keep the punctuation and spelling that carry feeling, because the voice uses them: exclamation and question marks, trailing dots, stutters like "W-wait" and stretched words like "Nooo".
- type says what kind of text it is:
  - "dialogue": words a character says aloud.
  - "thought": a character's inner thoughts, usually a cloud-shaped, fuzzy or dark bubble with no tail.
  - "narration": a caption box that tells the story.
  - "system": a game-style window or notification (quest, skill, item, stat, level up, warning).
  - "reaction": a sound a character makes instead of words: a cough, shriek, groan, sigh, gasp, laugh, sob, pant or grunt ("Cough!", "Kyaa!", "Agh!", "Haah...", "Hahaha!", "Hic..."). It may sit inside a bubble or loose next to the character. A sentence about a sound ("I have a cough") is dialogue.
  - "sfx": a drawn sound effect of a thing, not a voice (stylised lettering that is part of the artwork, like "BOOM", "slash", "thud").
  If the reply format does not offer a type, use "dialogue" for thought and reaction, and "narration" for system.
- When one bubble holds a sound and words, like "Cough! I'm fine", list them as two entries in order, each with that bubble's box: the reaction, then the words.
- sound, when the reply format has it: for a reaction, the sound it is: cough, shriek, groan, sigh, gasp, laugh, sob, pant or grunt. Use "" for everything else.
- tone is how the line should sound, and intensity how strongly it shows (1 mild, 2 clear, 3 extreme). Judge both from the face, what is happening and the words, using the bubble shape as a clue rather than a rule:
  - jagged, spiky or burst bubbles, big bold letters: shouting or strong emotion; a burst or huge letters is intensity 3.
  - wobbly or wavy outlines, shaky letters: scared, weak or crying.
  - dashed outlines, tiny bubbles or tiny letters: whispering; a mutter is intensity 1.
  - cloud-shaped or fuzzy bubbles: thinking when calm; a fuzzy black or jagged thought keeps its feeling (shouting, scared...).
  - icicles, drips, or a black bubble with white letters: cold or menacing.
  - soft round bubbles with hearts, flowers or sparkles: happy, teasing or embarrassed.
  - square or electronic outlines, or a zig-zag tail: a voice over a phone, radio or speaker; usually neutral or serious.
  A reaction takes its tone from the scene: "Kyaa!" facing danger is scared, among cheering friends excited; "Agh!" after a hit is pained, after a mistake angry; "Haah..." after a complaint is tired. Narration and system boxes are usually neutral.
- speaker is who says it, from the art: "narrator" for narration boxes, captions and system windows, otherwise the character's apparent age and gender (man, woman, boy, girl, old man, old woman), or "creature" for monsters and animals. Someone human-shaped with monstrous traits (a vampire, demon, elf or beast-woman, a long tongue, fangs, horns) is still a man, woman, boy or girl by how they look and sound: "creature" is only for beasts and monsters with no human form. Judge age and gender from the art for every line, even when you cannot name the person. A known character's type below is a guide for when you cannot see them; someone you can see who is clearly a different gender or age (a girl where the list says man) is not that character, so do not give them that label. For a voice from off panel or a bubble with no one drawn beside it, use the conversation: the person being answered, who is in the scene, and the line itself ("she", "Miss", how they speak). "unknown" and a wrong "man" both read with a man's voice, so a woman speaking off panel must still be "woman" or "girl"; use "unknown" only when there is no clue at all.
- character labels the person speaking, so each one can keep their own voice. The series' main character is always "hero", whatever they wear or are called. Only the main character of the whole series is hero: when the story follows someone else for a while (a side character's episode), label that person by their name, not hero. For anyone else, use their name when the story gives it (in dialogue, a name box or a caption). Otherwise use a short description of a look that stays the same, from face and hair rather than clothes or armour, like "black-haired swordsman" or "girl with twin braids". Someone in a new outfit, helmet or disguise is still the same character and keeps their label. Follow the bubble's tail to the speaker. Use "" for narration, for crowds, and when you cannot tell who is speaking. Never guess between two characters: "" is better than the wrong one.
- look: for a character not in the known list below, on their first line only, a few words on what stays the same (gender, rough age, hair, face). Otherwise "".
- Leave out watermarks, scanlator or website credits, page numbers and ads.
- Include bubbles cut off by the top or bottom edge of a slice, with the part you can read.
- box is [left, top, right, bottom] of the bubble as fractions from 0 to 1 of that slice's width and height.
Return every slice index, with an empty list for a slice that has no text.
